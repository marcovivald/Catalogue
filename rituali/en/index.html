HeritageOud · Bakhour · Luban

Heritage.

Before bottles, there were wood, resin and fire.

For millennia Arabia perfumed itself with what it burned. Frankincense from Oman and Yemen travelled with the caravans of the Incense Route; from India and South-East Asia came oud, the dark resinous wood that forms when the Aquilaria tree defends itself from a wound. The resin takes decades to mature: this is why oud has always been worth more than gold.

In the homes of the Gulf, bakhour has never gone out. The mabkhara passes from hand to hand among guests, smoke rises through the folds of robes and hair, the house is scented before a celebration, a wedding, a Friday. It is not a fashion: it is a gesture of welcome handed down from mother to daughter, from father to son.

Aquilaria

The tree oud comes from: its resin forms slowly, over decades.

AMAR MAISON03
Arabian layeringHow perfume is worn in the Gulf

In layers.

In Arabia perfume is not simply sprayed: it is built, one layer upon another.

01

Smoke

Bakhour on the mabkhara: the smoke wraps the body, the hair and the clothes held above the burner. It is the base, warm and deep, staying in the fabric for hours.

02

Oil

A drop of oud or musk oil on the beard, the wrists, behind the ear, and just a touch on certain garments, such as the collar or the ghutra. Oil does not evaporate: it fixes and warms.

03

Perfume

Last comes the spray: the fragrance settles over smoke and oil and becomes their voice. The layers merge into a single, personal trail that lasts from morning to night.

Each layer is beautiful alone. Together they become a signature.

AMAR MAISON04
GlossaryThe words of the ritual

The words.

Oudعود

The resinous wood of the Aquilaria and the oil distilled from it. In Arabic it simply means "wood".

Bakhourبخور

A blend of woods, resins, oils and musks pressed into pieces, to be burned on charcoal.

Lubanلبان

Frankincense, the resin of the Boswellia tree. The finest comes from Dhofar, in Oman.

Dehn al Oudدهن العود

Pure oud oil, distilled from the wood. Used in drops, alone or beneath a perfume.

Mabkharaمبخرة

The burner: glowing charcoal is placed in it, then wood or bakhour on top.

Tolaتولة

An old Gulf measure of about 12 ml: the traditional size for oils.
